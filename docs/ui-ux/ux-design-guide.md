# The Laws of UX: A Design Guide

Based on the work of Jon Yablonsky and presented by Joseph Todaro. Use these 19 psychological principles as a framework for building intuitive, efficient, and user-centered interfaces.

---

## Core UX Laws

* **Aesthetic Usability Effect:** Users perceive aesthetically pleasing designs as more usable than unattractive ones. High-quality visual design can mask minor usability issues.
* **Doherty Threshold:** Productivity increases when the interaction pace between computer and user is under 400ms, ensuring neither party has to wait.
* **Fitts’s Law:** The time to acquire a target depends on its distance and size. Important buttons should be large and placed in easily reachable areas.
* **Hick’s Law:** Decision-making time increases with the number and complexity of choices. Simplify tasks by breaking them into smaller, manageable steps.
* **Jakob’s Law:** Users spend most of their time on other sites. They prefer your site to function in familiar ways.
* **Miller’s Law:** The average person can only keep 7 (plus or minus 2) items in their working memory. Organize content into smaller groups or chunks.

---

## Visual Grouping and Perception (Gestalt Principles)

* **Law of Common Region:** Elements are perceived as a group if they share an area with a clearly defined boundary.
* **Law of Prägnanz:** People interpret complex images in their simplest form to reduce cognitive effort.
* **Law of Proximity:** Objects that are physically near each other tend to be perceived as a single group.
* **Law of Similarity:** The eye perceives elements that look similar (shape, color, size) as part of a related group or pattern.
* **Law of Uniform Connectedness:** Elements that are visually connected (by lines, colors, etc.) are perceived as more related than those without connections.

---

## Efficiency and System Design

* **Occam’s Razor:** Among competing hypotheses, the simplest one with the fewest assumptions is usually the best. Avoid over-engineering solutions.
* **Pareto Principle:** Roughly 80% of effects come from 20% of causes. Focus design efforts on the 20% of features that provide the most value to users.
* **Parkinson’s Law:** Any task will inflate until all available time is spent. Design interfaces that help users complete tasks quickly.
* **Postel’s Law:** Be liberal in what you accept (flexible user input) and conservative in what you send (standardized, clean output).
* **Tesler’s Law (Law of Conservation of Complexity):** Every system has an inherent amount of complexity that cannot be removed, only shifted from the user to the system.

---

## Memory and Attention

* **Serial Position Effect:** Users best remember the first and last items in a series. Place critical items at the beginning or end of lists.
* **Von Restorff Effect:** When multiple similar objects are present, the one that differs most from the rest is most likely to be remembered.
* **Zeigarnik Effect:** People remember uncompleted or interrupted tasks better than completed ones. Use this to encourage users to finish setup flows or goals.
